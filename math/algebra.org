#+begin_quote
Algebra is a branch of mathematics that deals with abstract systems,
known as algebraic structures, and the manipulation of expressions
within those systems. It is a generalization of arithmetic that
introduces variables and algebraic operations other than the standard
arithmetic operations, such as addition and multiplication.
#+end_quote

** Clifford Algebra
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       aa739e26-baef-44b8-ae09-3162df3e766a
:CREATED:  <2026-01-05 Mon 19:15>
:END:
:EDGES:
[2026-01-05 Mon 19:15] -- [[wikipedia:Clifford algebra][wiki]]
:END:
#+begin_quote
A Clifford algebra is an algebra generated by a vector space with a
quadratic form, and is a unital associative algebra with the
additional structure of a distinguished subspace. As K-algebras, they
generalize the real numbers, complex numbers, quaternions and several
other hypercomplex number systems.
#+end_quote

- The most familiar Clifford algebras, the orthogonal Clifford
  algebras, are also referred to as (pseudo-)Riemannian Clifford
  algebras, as distinct from symplectic Clifford algebras

- Clifford algebras are also sometimes referred to as geometric
  algebras, most often over the real numbers

*** Eigenvector
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       fa086b68-f270-4bab-82da-ff78844e6bb9
:CREATED:  <2025-05-26 Mon 19:03>
:AKA: characteristic vector
:END:
:EDGES:
[2025-05-26 Mon 19:03] -- [[wikipedia:Eigenvalues and eigenvectors][wiki]]
:END:
#+begin_quote
A vector that has its direction unchanged (or reversed) by a given
linear transformation. an eigenvector v of a linear transformation T
is scaled by a constant factor \lambda when the linear transformation is
applied to it: Tv = λv. The corresponding eigenvalue, characteristic
value, or characteristic root is the multiplying factor λ (possibly
negative).
#+end_quote

- The eigenvectors and eigenvalues of a linear transformation serve to
  characterize it

A /measure of roughness/, or more specifically *fractal dimension*
introduced in 1918 by Felix Hausdorff.

- single point = 0
- line segment = 1
- square = 2
- cube = 3
