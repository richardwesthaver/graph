*** Prisoner's Dilemma
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       6ae0c23a-7bad-4d49-a816-ebe403a65009
:CREATED:  <2025-10-16 Thu 18:45>
:END:
:EDGES:
[2025-10-16 Thu 18:45] -- [[wikipedia:Prisoner's Dilemma][wiki]]
:END:
- two-player game
- thought experiment involves two 'rational agents' with two choices: cooperate (stay silent) or defect (testify)
- /typical contemporary version/
  #+begin_quote
  Two members of a criminal gang are arrested and imprisoned. Each
  prisoner is in solitary confinement with no means of speaking to or
  exchanging messages with the other. The police admit they don't have
  enough evidence to convict the pair on the principal charge. They
  plan to sentence both to a year in prison on a lesser
  charge. Simultaneously, the police offer each prisoner a Faustian
  bargain. If he testifies against his partner, he will go free while
  the partner will get three years in prison on the main charge. Oh,
  yes, there is a catch ... If both prisoners testify against each
  other, both will be sentenced to two years in jail. The prisoners
  are given a little time to think this over, but in no case may
  either learn what the other has decided until he has irrevocably
  made his decision. Each is informed that the other prisoner is being
  offered the very same deal. Each prisoner is concerned only with his
  own welfare—with minimizing his own prison sentence.
  #+end_quote
  - possible outcomes:
    - If A and B both remain silent, they will each serve one year in
      prison.
    - If one testifies against the other but the other doesn’t, the
      one testifying will be set free while the other serves three
      years in prison.
    - If A and B testify against each other, they will each serve two
      years.

The /iterated Prisoner's dilemma/ gained popularity thanks to
Axelrod's book /The Evolution of Cooperation/ where he reports on the
results of a N-step prisoner's dilemma tournament he organized.

interesting strategies include:
- tit-for-tat :: the winner (in the simplest of cases...), always
  cooperate until opponent defects, then defect until opponent
  cooperates. The choice on whether to restart cooperation is based on
  a property called /forgiveness/. 
  - Typically the most *robust* strategy
- multi-agent :: when /communication/ comes into play, for example if
  you are able to submit multiple colluding strategies, as
  Southamption University in England did at the 20th-anniversary
  iterated prisoner's dilemma competition.. you can develop a /minmax/
  strategy which benefits a select few. The Southampton strategy
  (2004) took the top 3 spots by submitting 60 programs to the
  competition. These programs identified eachother through the
  /ten-move dance/ - a handshake which defected unrecognized opponents
  and funneled points to co-conspirators.
- bayesian nash equilibrium
- monte carlo
- win-stay, lose-switch :: face with failure to cooperate, player
  switches strat next turn.
- grim :: a /trigger strategy/ - basically tit-for-tat but no forgiveness.

- Stochastic iterated prisoner's dilemma
  - strategies specified in terms of /cooperation probabilities/
  - memory-n strat = fn of most recent /n/ encounters
  - memory-1 strat = fn of last encounter specified by 4 probabilities
    (p(xx) p(xy) p(yx) p(yy))
    - called /deterministic/ if all probabilities == 1,0
    - for example, tit-for-tat: =(1 0 1 0)=
    - win-stay,lose-switch: =(1 0 0 1)=
  - has been shown for any memory-n strategy there is a corresponding
    memory-1 strategy

- Zero-determinant strategies
  - 2012, William H. Press, Freeman Dyson

- Asymmetric prisoner's dilemmas
  - alibi game
